Experience / Dovenmuehle

Dovenmuehle · In-house

I made agreements usable for the people doing the work.

For nearly ten years I was the primary lawyer for Dovenmuehle's purchasing-side commercial and technology agreements. I negotiated them, explained what the terms meant for the planned work, and built the documents and processes that made the next agreement easier.

Role
Corporate Counsel from January 2020; Staff Attorney before that
Dates
February 2017 to October 2026
Scope
Purchasing-side commercial and technology agreements, from first review through termination
The work

The document has to fit the work people plan to do.

I handled master services agreements, statements of work, confidentiality and data-protection terms, riders and the documents around them, from review and negotiation through signature, amendments and termination.

Most of the job was translation. A business lead wants to know whether a vendor relationship is safe to start, what it will cost if it goes wrong, and how to get out if it has to end. I worked with business, technical, compliance and leadership colleagues to answer those questions in terms of their actual plans, and laid out the options when there was a choice to make.

The issues that came up most: liability, indemnity, intellectual property, service commitments and termination, plus audit rights, insurance, subcontracting and continuity.

Practical consequences

Every clause answers a business question.

The termThe business question
Limitation of liabilityIf this goes wrong, how much can we recover, and how much could we owe?
IndemnityIf someone sues over the vendor's work, who pays, and does the cap apply?
Intellectual propertyWho owns what gets built, and what can we keep using if we part ways?
Service commitmentsWhat happens when the service goes down, and what do we get when it does?
Audit rights and insuranceHow do we confirm the vendor is doing what it promised, and who covers a loss?
SubcontractingWho else will touch our work or our data?
Termination and continuityHow do we get out, and how does the work keep going if the vendor can't?

Process improvement

Then I made the next agreement easier.

The same questions came back on every deal. So I built the pieces that let us answer them faster and more consistently.

The contract rider

I created the company's contract rider: its own terms, ready to attach to a vendor's agreement, so a negotiation could start from our position.

The negotiation reference

I built and actively used a reference of preferred positions, the fallback language that could still work, and the reasoning behind each. A concession became a decision with a reason behind it, and the next negotiation started where the last one left off.

NDA documents and process

I developed the company's NDA documents and the intake, review and signature process around them: standard documents for routine requests, and a set path for the ones that needed a lawyer's review.

Access to vendors' assurance reports

Assurance reports are the independent audits vendors share about their controls. I made getting access to them a repeatable process by lining up the participants, the required documents and the routing.

  1. RequestWhat's being asked for?

    The purpose and the people involved.

  2. RequirementsWhat needs to be in place?

    The documents and any open questions.

  3. RoutingWho handles the next step?

    A clear handoff, and the decision that's left.
