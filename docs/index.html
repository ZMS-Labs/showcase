What I’m building,
and why.

See the work

I’m Zach Stern. I work with contracts. These are projects I’m building around contracts, writing, games and the practical problems I run into with AI tools.

AI tools write the code. I decide what each project is for and check what comes back.

Each one has a question behind it. If a vendor reports a data incident, which parts of the agreement decide who has to be told? How would AI models approach building a factory in Satisfactory, and would it actually work in the game? Some projects are further along than others, and each case study says what works so far.

If you only have a few minutes, start with Steno, SaveBench and Epistemic Skills.

Questions and conversations are welcome through my GitHub profile.

Steno · Contract review

A contract workstation that keeps its context.

One contract question can pull the notice terms, the indemnity and the liability limits into the same review. I wanted Steno to follow a question like that across the agreement and back to the words that answer it.

Prototype ·archived design, plus two drafting checks recorded on made-up clauses

SaveBench · Factories in Satisfactory

An AI model designs the factory. The game measures it.

I wanted to see what a factory designed by an AI model would produce in Satisfactory, with the running game doing the measuring. On 9 September 2026 the measuring tool failed its own check first, and after a repair the design fell short. On 13 September, factories designed by AI models passed a harder challenge in the game, with faster belts allowed. A newer test, where the factory has to keep producing while nobody touches it, has no pass yet.

Recorded experiment · a factory an AI model designed missed its targets in two of three measuring windows, and the miss is kept; other designs have passed

See the case study
Epistemic Skills · Checking an AI agent’s work

Seventeen written methods for AI agents.

An agent (an AI tool that carries out a multi-step task on its own) will sometimes name a cause it never checked, or call a fix done before it has taken effect. Each method tells the agent when to use it, what to hand back and when to stop, and the source is public.

Public source ·public releases, and one recorded use on this site (19 September 2026) with a check anyone can rerun

Gridiron · Game commentary

Record the play, then call it.

I want the commentary for a football video game to follow what happened on the field. The work behind the interface

What I try
to get right.

Keep the decisions with a person.

When I direct AI tools, the steps that can’t be undone wait for my approval. In Steno’s design, a model can sort documents and suggest clause categories, but it never decides what changes in a contract; fixed rules and the person reviewing it do. In Neuraxic, nothing counts as settled in the story until the author accepts it.

Show the choices on the screen.

I want the layout to show someone what they can change and what still needs their judgment. In Krewcible every choice stays in a visible list where it can be taken back, and Fleet Orchestrator’s main screen is meant to show which agent is working and which one is waiting on a decision.

Say what works so far.

I distinguish a design prototype from a working component or a recorded result. A failed result stays on the record too, like SaveBench’s missed delivery targets. See how to read this portfolio.
