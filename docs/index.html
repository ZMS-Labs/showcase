In-house practice

Every clause answers a business question.

My job was to make sure people had the answer before they signed, in terms of the work they actually planned to do.

  1. Limitation of liability

    If this goes wrong, how much can we recover, and how much could we owe?

    A cap is a number until something breaks. Then it's the difference between a manageable problem and one the business has to absorb.

  2. Indemnity

    If someone sues over the vendor's work, who pays?

    Indemnity and the liability cap have to be read together. Whether one sits inside the other changes the answer.

  3. Intellectual property

    Who owns what gets built, and what can we keep using if we part ways?

    That question is cheapest to settle before the work starts.

  4. Service commitments

    What happens when the service goes down, and what do we get when it does?

    An uptime promise matters only if it comes with a remedy the business would actually use.

  5. Termination

    How do we get out, and what happens to our data and the work in progress?

    Exit terms decide whether ending a relationship is routine or a crisis.

Tishler & Wald · Litigation

One bankrupt equipment lessee. More than 100 lenders claiming its equipment.

After Equipment Acquisition Resources went bankrupt in 2009, the equipment it left behind was claimed by more than 100 lenders and leasing companies. For our creditor client, I helped design and run a claims process in Cook County chancery court, outside the bankruptcy case.

I drafted the complaint that started it, coordinated service on the parties, worked with claimants' lawyers and kept the asset and claim records straight as they changed. The court authorized an auction, and every claim followed the equipment into the sale proceeds.
