ZMS Labs · How I work with AI tools

Some people let the wall of text fly by. I read it.

AI tools write the code. I decide what each project is for and check what comes back.

ZMS Labs is the name I use for my independent projects, separate from my legal work. I use AI tools for research, design, testing and writing too, including first drafts of these pages. I direct the work and review it with a critical eye.

In SaveBench, an AI model earned the game's milestones by feeding machines by hand and never built a conveyor belt, even after hand transfers started costing game time. At first I called that a flaw in the model. Then I decided the problem was the test: SaveBench measures factory design, not whether an AI chooses to automate. So the test became a production quota the factory has to keep meeting while nobody touches it.

Other people's libraries, tools and research are part of the work too, and each case study credits them. Every project page says what's real, what's sample content and what hasn't been tested yet.

How this grew

In January 2026, I was using Claude Code to work on my Home Assistant setup. Soon I was asking whether it could help on my other machines. That expanded into shared work across devices, gaming projects, a constitution for my AI agents, and quite a few revisions to how the whole thing should work.

I enjoy being able to take an idea, work through the possibilities, and get far enough to actually try it. I also want to understand what the assistants are doing and whether their results hold up. Today that means working with cloud and local agents, and building toward Fleet Orchestrator: one place where I can see the team at work, direct what comes next, and enjoy watching it happen.

A few words the projects use.

AI agent
An AI tool that carries out a multi-step task on its own, such as changing code and running tests.
Matter
In Interleaf: one contract with its related documents and open questions.
Hallmark
In Interleaf: the emblem that shows a grade and, separately, what kinds of evidence back it up. It's modeled on the stamp an assayer strikes into metal.
Surface Bridge
In Fleet Orchestrator: the part that turns a decision about an agent's next step into commands for whichever AI tool is running it, and reports back.
Canon
In Neuraxic: the version of a story's facts the author has accepted. Everything else is a proposal.
Tome and loose leaf
In Neuraxic: a tome groups a story world's ideas, prose and claims; a loose leaf is an idea that hasn't found its place yet.
Fingerprint
A short code computed from a file's exact contents (a SHA-256 hash). Change one byte and the fingerprint changes.
