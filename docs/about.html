About

I’m Zach Stern.

This is a collection of things I’m building. Some come from my legal work. Others come from writing, games, or trying to get AI tools to follow through on a task.

Every case study names what is real, what is synthetic, and what remains untested. See Evidence & attribution.

Steno

I work with contracts. I wanted a way to ask what an agreement would actually do in a particular situation, then follow the answer back to the language that supports it.

If a vendor reports a data incident, who needs to be told? Who is responsible? How do the liability terms affect the answer? Those questions can involve several parts of an agreement at once.

That’s what I’m working on with Steno: a contract workstation that keeps the document, its connections and the questions under review together. It’s a personal experiment, still in development. The legal decisions stay with the person reviewing the contract.

Epistemic Skills &
Fleet Orchestrator

I wanted agents to investigate problems properly, use the tools available to them and check whether their changes worked. Epistemic Skills grew around those expectations.

Fleet Orchestrator tackles a related problem: keeping track of several agents, their tasks and their handoffs. The cockpit and Surface Bridge are designed to make that work visible and give the operator a way to direct it.

Stories, characters
and factories

Neuraxic comes from wanting a place where a story’s prose, people, places and rules can develop together. Being able to try a different version without losing track of what I’d already decided was what I was after. Krewcible explores character design through choices you can edit and recombine.

With SaveBench, I wanted to see how AI models would approach building a factory in Satisfactory. What would they design? What would they change after a failed attempt? Would the factory actually work in the game? The layouts and revisions are part of what makes the experiment interesting to me.

Three supporting cases sit alongside these. ZMS Canvas is my fork of PenEcho, working on recovery when a save fails or versions conflict. Enaction keeps a fictional role from rewriting the person using it, including whose memory changes. Poiesis is a shared generation service that identifies returned files and limits how long they stay available.

How I build

I set the direction and work through the designs with AI tools. I use them extensively for code, research, writing and testing. Other people’s libraries, tools and projects are part of the work too, and are credited alongside it.

The case studies show the interfaces, explain the choices and say what has been tested. Some are prototypes; others show a working component or a specific result. There’s still plenty to do.

The words the projects use.

A few terms come from the projects themselves. Each one keeps a fixed meaning across the case studies.

  • matter (Steno): a case or engagement context that groups a contract, its relationships and review questions.
  • Hallmark (Steno): an assayer-style emblem showing a modeled grade and its evidence marks.
  • Surface Bridge (Fleet Orchestrator): the layer translating coordination decisions into a chosen runtime’s sessions or API calls.
  • tome (Neuraxic): a storyworld grouping that holds its related ideas, prose and claims.
  • loose leaf (Neuraxic): an element that has not yet found its place.

Independent, personally directed projects developed with AI assistance. Each case study identifies the work shown and the evidence behind its claims.
