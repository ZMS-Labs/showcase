Zachary M. Stern · Commercial counsel & independent creator

Make difficult work
easier to examine.

I’m Zachary M. Stern. My professional background is in commercial legal practice, with in-house work focused on vendor and technology agreements and practical improvements to recurring contract work.

ZMS Labs is where I explore related questions through independent, AI-assisted projects: how can better tools help someone understand a situation, consider the available choices and make a decision they can explain?

Practical experience.
Reusable ways of working.

My legal work spans the life of an agreement, from initial review and negotiation to changes and termination. It combines close attention to the wording with an understanding of the relationship the contract needs to support.

Commercial judgment

Vendor and technology contracts bring privacy, data security, service commitments, intellectual property, indemnity and liability into the same conversation. The useful answer has to connect the terms to the business situation.

Tools for recurring work

I’ve created and maintained a negotiation playbook, reusable agreement templates and review procedures. These capture preferred positions, workable alternatives and the reasoning behind them.

Work across disciplines

I work with technology, compliance, vendor oversight and business colleagues to gather the relevant facts, explain material issues and assess available options.

From legal questions
to product questions.

In a negotiation, the question is often what a clause would mean in a particular situation. If a vendor reports a data incident, for example, how do notice duties, responsibility and liability provisions fit together?

Steno explores a workstation for asking those questions: connect contract language, relationships and explicit checks so a person can inspect the result and retain legal judgment. It is personal, experimental work, separate from my professional practice.

My broader interest is in useful technology that brings complex work within reach. That includes the ability to explore an idea, revise a design, understand a result and see what remains uncertain. The creative and technical projects here let me pursue those questions beyond contracts.

The thread across the projects.

Steno puts a clause beside the questions it raises. Gridiron separates an observed event from language about it. Krewcible makes creative choices editable. Epistemic Skills asks agents to choose useful methods and check their outcomes. Fleet Orchestrator examines when a workflow is allowed to proceed. SaveBench connects factory-design choices to evaluation. Neuraxic brings prose, world relationships and canon decisions into one authoring workspace. ZMS Canvas keeps edits recoverable when a save fails, Enaction makes fictional-role attribution explicit, and Poiesis examines artifact identity and content custody.

These are different domains with a shared concern: making important relationships, changes and uncertainties easier to inspect.
