About

I’m Zach Stern.

This is a collection of things I’m building. Some come from my legal work. Others come from writing, games, or trying to get AI tools to follow through on a task.

Steno

I work with contracts. I wanted a way to ask what an agreement would actually do in a particular situation, then follow the answer back to the language that supports it.

If a vendor reports a data incident, who needs to be told? Who is responsible? How do the liability terms affect the answer? Those questions can involve several parts of an agreement at once.

That’s what I’m working on with Steno: a contract workstation that keeps the document, its connections and the questions under review together. It’s a personal experiment, still in development. The legal decisions stay with the person reviewing the contract.

Epistemic Skills &
Fleet Orchestrator

I wanted agents to investigate problems properly, use the tools available to them and check whether their changes actually worked. Epistemic Skills grew around those expectations.

Fleet Orchestrator tackles a related problem: keeping track of several agents, their tasks and their handoffs. The cockpit and Surface Bridge are designed to make that work visible and give the operator a way to direct it.

Stories, characters
and factories

Neuraxic comes from wanting a place where a story’s prose, people, places and rules can develop together. I wanted to be able to try a different version without losing track of what I’d already decided. Krewcible explores character design through choices you can edit and recombine.

With SaveBench, I wanted to see how AI models would approach building a factory in Satisfactory. What would they design? What would they change after a failed attempt? Would the factory actually work in the game? The layouts and revisions are part of what makes the experiment interesting to me.

How I build

I set the direction and work through the designs with AI tools. I use them extensively for code, research, writing and testing. Other people’s libraries, tools and projects are part of the work too, and are credited alongside it.

The case studies show the interfaces, explain the choices and say what has been tested. Some are prototypes; others show a working component or a specific result. There’s still plenty to do.
