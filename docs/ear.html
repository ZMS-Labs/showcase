Commercial litigation / Tishler & Wald

I helped make a chancery
claims process work.

At Tishler & Wald, I helped devise and operationalize a multi-year state-court claims process for our client. We pursued value from abandoned equipment and challenged competing creditors’ claims. My contribution included drafting, investigation, working records and coordination; the court made the decisions.

Tishler & Wald represented our client creditor, which asserted a blanket lien, in litigation against creditors asserting competing interests. EAR and its principals were on the opposing side of the wider dispute. EAR was never my business or employer.

Why the process was unusual

A chancery process alongside
a bankruptcy proceeding.

EAR had entered bankruptcy. This was a separate state-court process, outside ordinary bankruptcy claims administration: equipment disputes were consolidated in chancery, an auction was authorized, and claims followed the equipment into the auction proceeds. I helped turn that unusual procedural setting into working records, court materials and coordinated steps for our client.

This is not a claim that I invented a first-ever procedure or administered every creditor neutrally. Our client asserted a blanket-lien position; competing parties asserted their own rights. The opinions do not establish that every competing interest was subordinate.
