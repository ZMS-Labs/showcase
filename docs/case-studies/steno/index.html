Evidence, provenance and limits+

What is real here

On 19 September 2026, two checks ran on four clauses entered by hand as structured data. Steno did not read a document file or build the clause connections itself. Neither check uses an AI model, and eFLINT, Catala and clingo did not run for this example.

The original JSON retains its recorded party labels. Only the presentation uses Party A and Party B; the remaining clause wording and recorded findings are unchanged. The amounts, the 24-hour period and the edits are illustrative, not recommended terms.

The workspace images and recording show an archived prototype; the Hallmark boards show a separate component study. All use made-up content, not real agreements or matters. Steno is unfinished, its source is private, and nothing here demonstrates a deployed legal product.

The new hero composition, chapter motion and page layout are original conceptual website presentation. They are not screenshots of Steno or evidence of new product behavior. No new model or drafting-check run was performed for this prototype.

Read the public research and build account

Steno started with contract questions and with published research. I had AI agents work through papers collected in Zotero, a reference manager. One recorded sweep ran 46 searches and had to look for evidence against the idea as well as for it. The agents then checked in Scite, a citation database, whether later papers supported or disputed them. Scite's quota ran out partway through, and the papers it hadn't reached were marked unverified.

At its center, Steno is designed to check an agreement's structured parts against a library of positions, using fixed rules that give the same result every time. Language models only help at the edges, for example sorting documents or putting findings into plain English. A model never decides what changes in a document, which redline to use, or whether a result goes out.

For some hand-picked clauses, the design adds three existing tools made by others, each for one narrow question: what duties a clause creates and whether one is breached (eFLINT), whether a hand-built model of the clause is sound (Catala), and whether an exception overrides an obligation (clingo). Recorded runs from May 2026 used each tool in the private build on one clause family, limitation of liability. Doing this for any clause in ordinary prose is future work.

The build used Epistemic Skills, the set of working methods for AI agents shown elsewhere on this site. Its adversarial review step, where AI reviewers try to break a claim, caught an earlier write-up overstating what Catala verifies; the correction is kept in the record.

Proposed edits

The edits point the incident notice to the Notices section and carve indemnity out of the general limit.

Notices

Notices shall be delivered by email to the notice address designated by each party.

Security Incident

Party A shall provide written notice of a security incident within 24 hours, in accordance with the Notices article.

Indemnity

Party A shall indemnify Party B against covered third-party claims, subject to a separate $300,000 limit.

Limitation of Liability

Aggregate liability shall not exceed $100,000, excluding indemnification obligations. The separate indemnity limit in Section 8 applies to those obligations.

No findings from the two checks.

After the edits, neither check flags anything. The two drafting patterns they look for are gone; whether the agreement works is still the reviewer's call.
