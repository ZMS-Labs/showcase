What went into this work.

Project direction
The legal-workflow problem, the matter-centered product direction, and the requirement to preserve human judgment. The showcase also reflects the owner's insistence that Steno's own interface design be visible.
Construction and assistance
The preserved interface came through a user-created Claude Design handoff. AI-assisted preparation rendered it with fictional content and separately exercised two existing drafting checks. Neither artifact establishes a finished legal product.

The question behind the workspace.

A reported vendor incident can bring notice obligations, indemnity language and liability limits into the same review. Reading each clause in isolation makes it harder to see how the wording interacts.

Steno's direction is a matter-centered contract-and-law workstation: structured information, explicit checks, traceable relationships and a person who retains judgment. It is a personal experimental project, not a completed legal service.

An interface with a visual grammar.

The preserved design uses translucent glass panels, a serif display face, restrained sans-serif controls and monospace metadata. The matter workspace gives the document and its context a stable home.

The Matter Map design has a Layers rail, a graph and an Inspector. Its specification assigns different meanings to visual channels so evidence and uncertainty can be read independently.

The underlying Steno application remains private. This case study publishes selected design evidence and an independently authored synthetic example.

A decision worth examining.

Give the design and the behavior their own evidence.

Constraint
A small drafting-check example could explain a rule, but it could not communicate the workspace's visual language or intended review flow.
Choice
Retain the authentic map and workstation prototype, then place the actual component-check example beside it with separate labels.
Tradeoff
Readers see two artifacts rather than an apparently integrated demonstration. That preserves what each can honestly establish.
Result
The design can be inspected at full resolution and in motion; the two-check result can be inspected as recorded data.

01 · Start with a fictional incident.

The example brings notice, indemnity and liability-cap language into one review. Its input is structured synthetic clauses and relationships.

Four relevant clauses

Notices §6, incident notice §7, indemnity §8, and the liability cap §9. Document ingestion and graph construction were outside this check.

02 · Examine the two findings.

The existing checks identify a notice-mechanics linkage issue and an indemnity/cap wording issue.

Two recorded findings

The useful next step is to inspect the wording and its relationships, not to equate a finding count with the agreement's overall quality.

03 · Make explicit illustrative edits.

Add a Notices cross-reference. State that the general $100,000 cap excludes indemnity, with a separate $300,000 limit for that obligation.

Zero findings from the same two checks

The recorded rerun no longer produces those findings. The proposed figures are fictional illustration, not recommended negotiating positions.

04 · Keep the human questions open.

Someone still needs to assess clock triggers, claim coverage and risk allocation in context.

A narrower result than approval

Passing these checks does not establish legal sufficiency, agreement approval, or current end-to-end product operation.
