Start with a situation.

A focused question is more useful than a general request to “review the contract.”

The fictional assignment

A customer relies on a vendor for a service. After a reported incident, a reviewer needs to understand the agreement’s notice mechanics and how its indemnity and liability language is drafted.

The excerpts below were written for this case study. They are not adapted from a real agreement or a private negotiation.

Questions to carry into the review

  1. Does incident notice point to the correct delivery mechanism?
  2. Does the indemnity wording leave a consequential ambiguity?
  3. How is the liability limit expressed?
  4. What starts the notice clock, and which claims are covered?

03

Make the reasoning inspectable.

The engineering choices matter because the consequences belong to the reviewer.

§

Keep the words in view

Attach a finding to its clause and cross-reference. A reviewer can inspect the actual wording rather than accept a detached summary.

↔

Expose the connection

A delivery mechanism elsewhere in the agreement can change how a notice obligation is drafted. The connection is part of the evidence.

⋯

Preserve the unanswered part

Deterministic rules can detect selected patterns. Interpretation, factual investigation, commercial choice, and release authority remain with a person. Here, even the revised incident clause leaves the notice clock’s starting event unspecified.

About Steno

Steno is a personal, experimental contract-and-law workstation. Its aim is to connect document language, explicit checks, and supporting evidence in a form a lawyer can inspect. Development and operator acceptance remain unfinished. It is not represented as employer-deployed or commercially proven.

This case study was prepared with AI-assisted implementation and review. It presents a selected behavior and the design reasoning around it; it does not claim that every part of the broader product has been verified.
