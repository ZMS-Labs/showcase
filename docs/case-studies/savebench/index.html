SaveBench · Game-grounded factory evaluation

Design a factory.
Measure what it does.

SaveBench explores AI factory design in Satisfactory: give contestants a concrete challenge, inspect the layouts and choices they produce, then measure what those designs actually do in the game. The interesting result is an executable factory with a traceable design history.

A challenge has to become a working arrangement.

A useful factory challenge specifies more than “make something efficient.” It defines an output, starting resources and region, permitted equipment or recipes, and the measurement conditions. Contestants respond with production choices, machine placements, transport connections and revisions.

That makes spatial design consequential. Machines can be correctly named in a plan yet be badly connected, badly placed or unable to sustain the required output. The actual game supplies behavior that a convincing explanation, a valid file or a tidy diagram cannot establish on its own.

The project’s direction also values the design process: what a contestant publicly proposes, changes and retains. Those declared decisions and revision records are inspectable artifacts. They are not a reconstructed account of a model’s private reasoning.

Make design differences worth comparing.

Output rate is one question. Space, complexity, transport strategy and recipe choice can produce very different factories that pursue the same objective. SaveBench’s direction is to make those differences visible while keeping measured facts separate from judgments about architectural interest.

Production and footprint

A compact design and a sprawling design may meet the same target. The map gives the arrangement a visible form; measurements must establish what it actually delivers.

Transport and recipes

Connection strategy and standard versus alternate recipes change the design problem. The allowed choices belong in the challenge conditions before a comparison is interpreted.

Frozen conditions

Shared criteria make the comparison intelligible. Revising a target after seeing a result would change the question the contestants were answering.

Retained revisions

An earlier design can explain a later choice. Selection and replay make that history inspectable without pretending the interface has captured hidden thought.

These are product intentions and reasons for the interface. The prototype capture does not demonstrate a completed general-purpose contest platform or establish a ranking across models.

The map is the place to inspect the work.

The original UX keeps a selected contestant and revision in a map-centered workspace. Challenge conditions, declared activity and comparison views give that selection context. A factory camera helps frame the arrangement, while revision selection and replay let an observer examine how the visible design changes.

A real experiment: test the instrument first.

The interface explains the intended experience. This separate case examines two retained historical trials from 9 September 2026: a known-working control first reported zero delivery, then an instrument repair allowed a valid failing result. These authored diagrams and tables explain those trials. They display recorded results; they do not run the game or simulate a new contestant.

Step 1 of 5

The known-flow control reports zero.

The control fixture should deliver both products. Instead, it records zero copper ingots and zero concrete in each of two 61-second game windows.

The trial stops as INCONCLUSIVE before obtaining a contestant game observation. Its contestant measurements remain absent, rather than becoming a zero-production score.

Repair the instrument, then test both directions.

The investigation found an inventory-filter defect in the save-building adapter. Its encoding was corrected against game-written containers.

In the new trial, the known-flow control delivers both materials. Removing the connection yields zero for both. Positive delivery and an expected zero now provide complementary checks.

Keep the design and targets. Disclose the rebuild.

The design, task and generation manifest are byte-identical across the two trials. The design revision and challenge identity also match.

The compiled save bytes are different. The adapter and agent-tool implementation changed, the instrument identity changed, and the candidate was rebuilt. This is a repaired evaluation of the same design—not a comparison using identical binaries or an unchanged instrument.

Judge every window against both targets.

The factory delivers copper ingots and concrete. But the rule requires at least 20 copper ingots/min and 15 concrete/min in every measured window.

Copper misses in the first window. Both products meet their targets in the second. Concrete misses in the third. Later copper production does not cancel an earlier miss.

Retain the miss instead of moving the target.

The repaired trial is scored FAIL against the frozen per-window targets. The earlier INCONCLUSIVE attempt remains a separate record.

The failing result is useful precisely because it is specific: the factory produced both materials, but it did not satisfy every required delivery window. It is not a general statement about the model or the factory’s entire operating life.

The evidence supports the decision that was actually made. No invented aggregate score, relaxed threshold or current model-performance claim is added to this historical experiment.

Choice

Reason

Boundary

Choice
Require both output minima in every window.
Reason

Purpose and contribution.

Personal direction
Defined the benchmark around interesting factory-design choices, competing goals and outcomes demonstrated by the actual game. Later planning explicitly refocused the project on factory design rather than merely discovering automation.
Work shown here
The original challenge/comparison interface rendered with synthetic data, alongside an existing experiment’s instrument repair, controls and retained result. Product construction and visual preparation used AI assistance; this case does not establish sole personal authorship of the interface, adapter, evaluator or tests.

A historical result with a precise boundary.

The case was checked against retained trial reports and game-observation verdicts from 9 September 2026. The measurement summary preserves the exact numeric rates, targets and per-window outcomes.

Checked during preparation
Control outcomes and rates; the absent first-trial contestant observation; byte-identical design, task and manifest; changed candidate and instrument identities; and a recomputation of each window’s target result.
Not rerun for this page
No new game session, model contest, cluster action or paid trial. This page does not claim a fresh validation of today’s implementation.

What this experiment does not establish

It is not a model ranking, a completed autonomous progression campaign or a claim that every supported factory design works. It does not measure long-run performance outside the recorded windows.

The experiment’s diagrams and tables are authored explanations of retained evidence. The separate interface captures show a prototype with synthetic fixtures. No game screenshot, terrain/map tile, save file, application source or private operational detail is published here.
