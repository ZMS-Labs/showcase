[0:00] SaveBench. Can an AI build a working factory in Satisfactory, by the game's own rules? This is where it stands on November 19, 2026.

[0:06] The first milestone. Start in a blank world and earn milestone 1-1. It costs 100 Iron Plate, 100 Iron Rod and 200 Concrete. It must be done the way a player does it: ore comes from miners on the map's ore nodes, parts move between machines by belt, and hands are only for getting started. The game is the judge: the milestone counts when the live game accepts the payment and launches it. Script is a reference run, a fixed list of steps we wrote, and it is not an AI. Four AI models have tried, each choosing every step by itself on a small daily budget: deepseek-v4-flash, Claude Opus 5.5, GPT-6.1 Sol and, from November 18, Claude Fable 5.2.

[0:20] The script run, run BL, November 14, shown as a diagram that is not to scale. The script clears the rock on an iron node by hand and builds a Miner Mk1 on it, powered by a Biomass Burner. Belts carry ore to the Smelter, ingots to the Constructor and plates into the bank. 324 Iron Plate arrive by belt, where 320 were wanted. The Constructor then switches to Iron Rod, and 202 arrive, where 200 were wanted. A second line starts at a Miner Mk1 on a stone node. The game refuses a belt that is too long, so the stone passes through four containers before a Constructor turns it into Concrete: 203, where 200 were wanted. No part was moved by hand between machines. In this run the burner was still refuelled by hand; that gap closed on November 16. The finished Concrete is carried to the bank once, to pay. The game accepts the payment and launches milestone 1-1. 59 checks of 59 passed. This is a script run, not an AI.

[0:46] The second script run, run BP, November 16, shown as a diagram. Milestone 1-2 costs 150 Iron Plate, 150 Iron Rod and 300 Wire. The script builds a copper line first, at a copper node 268 metres from the bank: a Miner Mk1, a Smelter and a Constructor making Wire. The first 48 Wire are carried to the bank to pay for the iron line, a declared carry. The iron line runs into the bank by belt: 196 Iron Plate where 190 were wanted, then 170 Iron Rod where 170 were wanted. The copper line gets its container and 304 Wire arrive in it by belt, where 300 were wanted. The finished Wire is carried to the bank once, to pay. The game accepts the payment and launches milestone 1-2. 52 checks of 52 passed. In a separate run the same evening, a burner ran on fuel fed by belt with no hand refuel.

[1:03] The DeepSeek run, run 4, November 14, shown as a diagram. DeepSeek cleared the rock and built a Miner Mk1, a Smelter, a Constructor, storage, a Biomass Burner and two belts, and wired the burner to the miner. It had spent all its Iron Rod. The last belt was refused because it cost 16 Iron Plate and the bank held 1. Power poles also cost Iron Rod, so the Smelter and Constructor never got power; for the poles this is inferred. Our harness told the model only that the placement was refused, so it spent 15 of its 34 replies looking for clear ground. The run stopped at its budget of 95 cents and the milestone was not earned. The fault was in our harness, not in how the model played. The fix was live for run 5 on November 16. In run 5 the model was told what it could not afford, but 14 of its 30 replies were used up thinking and produced no action, and it did not finish either. DeepSeek has now tried five times.

[1:13] The Claude Opus 5.5 run, run 6, November 16, shown as a diagram. Claude Opus 5.5, an AI model, plays milestone 1-1 by itself from a blank world. It looked up an iron node, cleared the rock by hand, and built a Biomass Burner, a Miner Mk1, a Smelter and a Constructor, joined by belts. It tried to belt the Constructor to the bank and was refused, because our tool list did not offer the bank as a belt target, so it built a storage box. It ran power to a stone node and built a second Miner Mk1, a second Constructor and a second storage box for Concrete. Machines made everything the milestone costs: 128 Iron Plate where 100 were needed, then 184 Iron Rod where 100 were needed after it switched the Constructor, and 248 Concrete where 200 were needed. No machine was fed by hand. The declared hand steps were clearing the two nodes, 200 biomass for the burner, and collecting the finished parts from storage to pay. The game accepted the payment and launched milestone 1-1, after 10 replies and 35 cents. This was the first milestone earned by an AI. The next day, November 17, Claude Opus 5.5 went on to earn milestones 1-2, 1-3 and 2-1, the first of tier 2, and a second AI model, GPT-6.1 Sol, earned milestones 1-1 and 1-2, each run starting from a blank world.

[1:36] The scoreboard. Script: milestone 1-1 on November 14 with every part from miners by belt; milestone 1-2 on November 16 the same way, then both again in one world; on November 17, after a first try that made no Screws, it earned milestone 1-3 in that same world with 304 Screws by belt, finishing all of tier 1 in one factory; and on November 18, in that same world, it earned milestones 2-1 and 2-5 in run BW and 2-2 in run BX, every part made by machines and moved by belt; and on November 19 it earned milestone 2-3 in run BZ, with 50 Rotors made by an Assembler fed by belts. The Assembler's own building cost included 4 Rotors and 8 Reinforced Iron Plate crafted by hand at the base's workbench, which the rules allow for buildings. AI models: Claude Opus 5.5 earned milestones 1-1 and 1-2 on November 16 and 17 with every part made by machines; Claude Opus 5.5 earned milestone 1-3 on November 17, where its machines made enough of everything but it had spent plates on belts, so it crafted 72 plates by hand before paying; Claude Opus 5.5 earned milestone 2-1 on November 17, the first tier 2 milestone, but it carried 184 ore and 274 ingots into its machines by hand and crafted 160 plates by hand at the base's workbench before paying; GPT-6.1 Sol earned milestones 1-1 and 1-2 on November 17 with every part made by machines, and its try at 1-3 ran out of time. All are verified from run records. The script has earned seven milestones in one world. Claude Opus 5.5 has reached 2-1, with hand help on 1-3 and 2-1, and GPT-6.1 Sol has reached 1-2, each milestone in its own blank world: four different milestones between them. DeepSeek has not finished one.

[1:44] How it got here. October 27: the concept video, before anything had been measured in the game. November 9: the first belt between two machines in the live game. November 12 and 13: Space Elevator phase one, a Miner Mk1 feeding a belt, then 300 plates by belt. November 14: milestone 1-1 by a real factory and the first copper Wire. November 16: milestone 1-2, a burner fed by belt, and an AI model earns milestone 1-1. November 17: the script finishes tier 1 in one world, AI models earn milestones 1-1, 1-2, 1-3 and 2-1, and a locomotive drives for the first time. November 18: the script earns milestones 2-1, 2-5 and 2-2, and in test worlds stocked for the check a coal power plant runs on its own, drones carry items for the first time, and an oil site makes Plastic and Petroleum Coke. November 19: the script earns milestone 2-3. Game actions checked in the live game rose from 5 of 51 on November 9 to 28 of 51 on November 11, 38 of 52 on November 14 and 42 of 52 on November 16. The last two counts are a work thread's own and were not read from a file.

[1:54] What is still ahead. The script has not built the Space Elevator's first phase: on November 19 it made 400 Iron Plate and 420 Iron Rod by belt, but it was 470 Concrete short. On November 18 two drones, paired the way the game pairs them, carried 38 items to a container; this was a check in a test world stocked for it, not part of an earned factory. Two tries the same day to carry cargo by vehicle between stations carried nothing. A Tractor drove 26 metres on autopilot on November 16, but no vehicle has carried cargo between stations yet. GPT-6.1 Sol's try at milestone 1-3 ran out of time with 140 of 300 Screws made. Claude Opus 5.5 reached tier 2 but carried some ore into machines by hand. Every AI milestone so far started from a blank world; next comes several milestones in one world. On November 17 a locomotive drove 57 metres along a new rail piece to the next station, but it cannot make the return trip yet and no train has carried cargo; six freight train tries on November 19 carried nothing, and the engine reported the stations unreachable.
